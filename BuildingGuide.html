Building Your Portfolio

The Building Guide will give you a clear starting point for developing the content that will shape your creative portfolio. Here, we'll figure out which projects to highlight, how to organize your work, and which tools can make your portfolio look polished and professional.

What's a Portfolio?

If you're new to a creative field or still in school, you might be wondering what actually belongs in a portfolio. The good news is that you don't need years of experience or dozens of projects to get started. A portfolio is simply a collection of your work that tangibly highlights your skills, interests, and strengths as a creative. In industries like graphic design, animation, brand marketing, film, videography, and UX/UI, portfolios often matter just as much as the resume. Schools, employers, and clients use them to see the quality of your work, your specific style, and whether you're a good fit for an opportunity in schooling, internships, freelance work, or other roles.

So, let's break down the process of building a portfolio!

Creating Your Content

One of the biggest misconceptions about portfolios is that every project needs to come from a job or internship. In reality, many students build their first portfolios using class assignments, personal projects, and other creative endeavors. If you're still developing your skills, focus on creating work that reflects your interests and the type of career you hope to pursue.

Where do portfolio projects come from?

  • Class Projects - Class assignments are often the easiest place to start. Design projects, IMC work, short films, and websites can all become strong portfolio pieces.
  • Personal Projects - Personal projects let you explore topics and creative styles that interest you. They also show commitment, versitility, and give you to freedom to present your own creative flair.
  • Mockup Client Work - If you don't have real clients yet, create your own project. Designing a logo, brand identity, or marketing campaign for a fictional company can still demonstrate valuable skills.
  • Group Projects and Clubs - Group assignments, student organizations, and creative clubs can provide portfolio-worthy experience. Just be sure to explain your role so viewers understand your contributions.
  • Internships and Early Work - Internships, freelance projects, and entry-level jobs will become valuable additions to your portfolio as you gain experience. Like with group projects, focus on the work that best represents your skillset and clearly describe how you contributed.

Industry Programs

While we'll go into more detail about each platform on Tools and Resources, it's good to become aqauinted with software/programs you'll likely encounter in several creative fields.

Abobe Photoshop

Adobe Photoshop is one of the most widely used creative programs for photo editing, digital artwork, image manipulation, and marketing graphics. It's commonly used in graphic design, photography, IMC/brand marketing, and content creation.

Adobe Illustrator

Adobe Illustrator is used to create logos, vector graphics, icons, typography, and brand assets. It's an industry standard in graphic design and branding

Adobe Premiere Pro

Premiere Pro is a professional video editing program used for films, commercials, social media content, and digital marketing campaigns. It's widely used throughout the film, videography, and content creation spheres, and experience with this program is often expected for intern or entry-level positions.

Adobe After Effects

After Effects specializes in motion graphics, animation, visual effects, and animated text, commonly used in marketing campaigns, adverts, and a variety of animated content.

Figma

Figma is a popular design tool used to create websites, mobile apps, and digital interfaces. It's especially useful for students interested in UX/UI, web design, or digital product design.

Canva

Canva is popular for its intuituve interface and used to create social media graphics, presentations, and marketing materials. It's a great starting point for those interested in communication studies, marketing, and content creation who want to create professional projects without a steep learning curve of other programs.

Toon Boom Harmony

Toon Boom Harmony is one of the most widely used programs in 2D animation. Creatives interested in animation can use it to create character animations, compositions, and character rigs while learning workflows similar to those used in professional studios.

Maya Autodesk

Maya is a leading software for 3D modeling and animation in film, television, and game development. Creatives pursuing animation, visual effects, or game design often learn Maya to create character models, environments, and animations used in professional production pipelines.

Don't have access to professional software yet? Many students start with free alternatives such as Blender, DaVinci Resolve, Photopea, Inkscape, and Audacity. While industry-standard tools are worth learning, strong portfolio projects matter more than the software used to create them.
